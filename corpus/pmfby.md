# Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) — Crop Insurance
Source: https://pmfby.gov.in (starter summary compiled from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradhan_Mantri_Fasal_Bima_Yojana and public PMFBY guides; replace with official operational guidelines PDF)

## What is PMFBY
PMFBY is the Government of India's crop insurance scheme, launched on 18 February 2016 and run by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare. It gives farmers financial support when their crops fail or are damaged by natural calamities, pests or disease. The official website is pmfby.gov.in.

## Who is eligible
All farmers, including sharecroppers and tenant farmers, who grow notified crops in a notified area during the season and have an insurable interest in the crop are eligible. Since Kharif 2020, enrolment is 100% voluntary for all farmers. Before that it was compulsory for loanee farmers who had crop loans or a Kisan Credit Card (KCC) account.

## Which crops are covered
Food crops (cereals, millets and pulses), oilseeds, and annual commercial or horticultural crops are covered. Perennial crops can be covered on a pilot basis where a standard yield estimation method exists. Only crops notified by the state government for that area and season are covered.

## Premium the farmer pays
The farmer pays only a small part of the premium, and the Central and State governments pay the rest:
- Kharif crops: 2% of the sum insured
- Rabi crops: 1.5% of the sum insured
- Annual commercial and horticultural crops: 5% of the sum insured

## Risks that are covered
PMFBY covers losses at these stages:
1. Prevented sowing, planting or germination: when deficit rainfall or bad weather prevents sowing.
2. Standing crop (sowing to harvest): yield losses from drought, dry spells, flood, inundation, pests, disease, landslides, natural fire, lightning, storm, hailstorm and cyclone.
3. Post-harvest losses: for up to two weeks (14 days) after harvest, for crops kept in the field to dry, against hailstorm, cyclone, cyclonic rain and unseasonal rain.
4. Localized calamities: damage to individual farms from hailstorm, landslide, inundation, cloudburst and natural fire due to lightning.
5. Add-on cover: crop loss due to wild animal attack, if the state chooses to provide it.

## What is not covered
Losses caused by war and nuclear risks, malicious damage, and other preventable risks are excluded.

## How to report crop loss (claim intimation)
For localized calamities and post-harvest losses, the farmer must report the crop loss within 72 hours of the damage. It can be reported through the toll-free Krishi Rakshak helpline 14447, the Crop Insurance app, the insurance company, the bank branch, or the agriculture department office. Missing the 72-hour window can lead to the claim being rejected. Keep the policy or application number and photos of the damaged crop ready.

## How losses are assessed
For widespread losses, the insurance unit is an area (usually a village or village panchayat for major crops), and the claim is calculated from crop cutting experiments that measure the actual yield against the threshold yield. For localized calamities and post-harvest losses, the loss is assessed for the individual farm.

## Enrolment deadlines
The cut-off date for enrolling (submitting the proposal) is usually 31 July for the Kharif season and 31 December for the Rabi season. Loans are usually sanctioned in April–July for Kharif and October–December for Rabi. The exact dates are notified by each state every season and may be different, so farmers should check with their bank, PACS, Common Service Centre (CSC) or pmfby.gov.in.

## How to enrol
Loanee farmers can enrol through the bank or PACS where they took the crop loan. Non-loanee farmers can enrol through a bank branch, a Common Service Centre (CSC), an authorised insurance agent, or online at pmfby.gov.in. The usual documents are an Aadhaar card, bank account details (passbook), land records or a tenancy/sharecropping agreement, and the sowing declaration.
